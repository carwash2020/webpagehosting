How to Use: Dashboard

This is the home base for the whole business — bookmark this one page. It's built to answer "what do I need to do right now" without a tap, top to bottom:

  • Next Job — whatever's next on today's schedule, sorted by priority, with a tap-to-call phone number and a tap-to-open-Maps address, a straight line into that job's own page, and Route today: one link that opens the whole day's stops as a Google Maps route.
  • Money Owed — Current and Overdue, split apart so a client who's simply not due yet never gets lumped in with one who needs a follow-up call. Every unpaid invoice is listed right there (overdue first) with a one-tap Mark paid — confirm, and it's recorded as paid in full.
  • Your week — the scoreboard under the daily actions. At the top, Start my day when you begin work and End my day when you're done (or use the clock button at the top of any page). Then a bar for each day, Monday to Sunday: the green part is the hours you worked — driving, estimates and the time between jobs included — and the orange part inside it is the time on a job's clock. Under the bars: hours worked, hours on the clock, jobs done and billed, with last week's number under each. Billed means invoices dated this week plus income you logged by hand; it shows only to accounts that can see finance, and so does Everyone this week, everyone's hours. Forgot to clock out? A shift left open more than 14 hours stops counting until you say when you finished — it suggests when your last job clock stopped. The shift and the job clock are separate: neither starts or stops the other.
  • Texts that write themselves — On my way on Next Job, the Text button on a job, or Text in its sheet on Jobs. You get the texts that job calls for, already written with the client's name: On my way or Running late (pick 10, 20, 30 or 45 minutes), Confirm the visit, a quick Parts run, All done. Edit it if you like and Send: it opens your own Messages. The job remembers the last one (“On my way sent 12 min ago”).
  • Remind — on any invoice that's due or late (on Money Owed, in an invoice's sheet on Invoices, and on the job). The reminder is already written: their first name, the invoice, what's still owed, how late it is, and — for an invoice on the client portal — where to pay by card. Change anything you like, then Text, Email, or Copy it; it goes out from your own phone. Each one is logged on the invoice (“Reminded 3 days ago”), and the next one is a notch firmer: friendly, then following up, then firm.
  • Rest of Today — whatever else is on today's schedule after the next job.
  • New job / Create invoice / Find client / Calendar / Quick charge / Log expense — the six daily actions, one tap each. Quick charge takes a card payment on the spot with no invoice; Log expense opens Finance on the Expenses tab with the receipt form ready. On a phone the bar at the bottom is Home, Jobs, the orange +, Clients, and Money (Invoices and Finance, with a switch at the top of either to hop between them); the grid button at the top right opens the rest (Route Planner, Runway, Contracts, Reviews, the Appliance Wiki, Settings). On a computer everything is in the sidebar.
  • Create anything — the orange + in the middle of the bar (or New at the top of the sidebar, or the N key) starts a job, invoice, quote, quick charge, expense, income entry, contact, contract, or review request from any page. Or type (or say, with the microphone) what you need at the top of it: “sink leak for Sarah tomorrow 2pm” opens a new job with Sarah Miller's details, the date, and the time filled in; start with invoice, quote, or expense for those (“invoice Tom $85”, “expense $48 Home Depot drain pump”). You check it and save it on the form, same as always. Search (Ctrl+K) offers the same thing as its top result. A client texted you? Copy it, then tap Paste a client’s text under the field, and it pulls out what's wrong, who, and when, and keeps the message itself in the job's notes. On Android, share the text straight to Triple H from Messages. On iPhone, a Shortcut can open /tools/workspace.html?quick= followed by the text. The home-screen icon's Quick add shortcut opens it too.
  • On the clock — tap Start the clock when you get to a job (on the job, on its card in Jobs, or on Next Job here) and a bar with the time ticking follows you to every page, and to your other devices once they sync. Stop adds the time to the job's hours and asks what's next: Done — create the invoice opens an invoice with the labor line already filled from those hours. Stopped by mistake? Keep the clock running puts it back. Starting another job's clock stops the first and keeps its time.
  • Search anywhere — the magnifier at the top right of every page (or Ctrl+K / ⌘K on a computer, or the Search row at the top of the sidebar) finds any job, contact, invoice, quote, or contract by name and jumps straight to it, and lists every action in the app (Log expense, New quote, Quick charge, Plan a route...) so you never have to remember which page something lives on. Type a few letters and pick.
  • Needs attention — the ops inbox, open by default: work requests, new leads, applicants, and online bookings that still need a response (an empty group hides itself, so you only see what's waiting); jobs due this week; clients overdue for a follow-up; Ready to invoice — jobs finished in the last 60 days with no invoice and no payment logged, oldest first, each with an Invoice button (the ··· covers “paid another way” and “no charge”); and invoices still unpaid, with paid ones folded away underneath. Tap the heading to collapse it if you'd rather — your choice is remembered on this device.
  • Business — everything you check occasionally rather than daily, each collapsed until you want it: Business Snapshot (revenue, expenses, net, outstanding), Analytics (job status, top vendors and clients, lead sources), Compliance & Documents (insurance, LLC/tax info, city licenses, secure files), and the Website Gallery Queue (photos starred in the Job Tracker, review-only). Backup & Restore lives in Dev Tools (Session tab).

A typical day, start to finish:

  1. Open the Dashboard: Next Job, Money Owed, and Needs attention are the first screen.
  2. Heading out? Tap Route today on the Next Job card for the whole day's driving in Google Maps.
  3. New job comes in? Tap New job — that opens Job Tracker with the add form ready. Use the microphone icon next to Notes to dictate instead of typing. Every dated job shows on the Calendar view automatically.
  4. On site, tap Done on the job's card or table row (or use the status dropdown for In Progress). Attach photos with the Photos button. Marking a job done asks what's next: Create invoice (the form opens filled from the job) or a pre-filled review request.
  5. Forgot to invoice one? It waits in Ready to invoice (and as To invoice on the Money Owed card) until it's billed, paid another way, or marked no charge.
  6. Got paid? Mark paid on the Money Owed card — two taps. Small job, no invoice needed? Quick charge takes the card right there.
  7. Bought parts? Log expense — photograph the receipt, pick the job, done. It counts against that job's profitability automatically.
  8. End of week: skim Needs attention for anything still waiting, and Compliance under Business for anything expiring soon.

Data is saved directly in your browser, and quietly kept in sync across your devices in the background — the small dot next to the sync status turns green when live updates are connected, meaning changes on another device (or a new lead coming in) show up here automatically. The small circular arrow button next to it forces an immediate refresh if you don't want to wait.

Install this as an app: iPhone/iPad — tap the Share icon in Safari, then "Add to Home Screen." Android — tap the ⋮ menu in Chrome, then "Install app." Computer — look for an install icon in the address bar. Once installed, it opens full-screen with its own icon, no browser bar.

Worth knowing: this whole suite is behind a real login — anyone hitting these pages without signing in gets redirected to a sign-in screen first. Each account has its own role (Owner, Developer, or Employee) controlling what it can see and do, not one shared login for everyone.
